# Delivery sequence

The coordinator drives this sequence. Each stage ends with evidence, a short owner-facing result
and a filled prompt for the next focused session. We keep the whole ambition visible, while
learning and building through complete small scenarios.

| Stage                                 | Work                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Evidence to move on                                                                                                                                           | Owner involvement                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0. Foundation                         | Public repository, GPT-only workflow, source pointers and inexpensive CI.                                                                                                                                          | Published repository, passing documentation checks, separate foundation review and current handoff.                                                           | Current authorization covers this preparation.                                                      |
| 1. Market and table needs             | Audit existing reports; map relevant products; collect public DM/player experiences with sources and counter-evidence. Inventory previous jobs and current workarounds.                                            | A coverage map, claim register and ranked problems, with uncertainty and access gaps visible.                                                                 | Only missing facts about their own group's use that would change direction.                         |
| 2. Experience and domain              | Describe the whole product briefly, then choose the first short encounter. Map its objects, permissions, events, rule sources and phone/DM journeys together.                                                      | One understandable scenario, concrete success criteria, rule examples and the expensive assumptions to test.                                                  | Confirm product intent from concrete examples, not a technical specification.                       |
| 3. Design exploration and feasibility | Compare leading products on that scenario. Produce two coherent visual directions, rough interactive prototypes and disposable tests for offline authority, duplicate actions, corrections and hidden information. | Side-by-side visual evidence; a working scenario; reproducible results for the costly technical assumptions. Iterate when either design or feasibility fails. | Choose between visual alternatives with a clear recommendation and explanation.                     |
| 4. Refine the experience              | Exercise the prototype with keyboard/touch, EN/IT, phone/desktop, recovery cases and separate agent review. Improve attention, density, clarity and game feel.                                                     | An approved direction and an observable interaction contract. Agent tests and optional human feedback are labeled separately.                                 | Judge a short playable demonstration. No standing obligation to recruit a DM every cycle.           |
| 5. Name, position and brand           | Once the experience and audience are clear, compare names, meaning, differentiation, visual identity and availability. Connect the brand to the product's actual value.                                            | A coherent brand proposal and checked availability; naming choice recorded.                                                                                   | Choose the final direction. Purchases or legal commitments require separate concrete authorization. |
| 6. Build and expand                   | Implement the approved scenario end to end with appropriate tests, running-app checks and fresh reviews; then choose the next scenario from the whole-product inventory.                                           | A reliable playable increment; regression checks and evidence of improvement.                                                                                 | Only material scope/taste decisions. Routine technical work is delegated.                           |
| 7. Rehearse transition and release    | Plan coexistence early, rehearse export/import/restore before meaningful player data accumulates, and prepare cutover, rollback and operating instructions.                                                        | Verified recovery and migration rehearsal; owner can see what will happen and undo what is reversible.                                                        | Explicit permission for real-data movement, deployment and release.                                 |

Stages 2–4 loop on a scenario; they are not a requirement to specify every future screen before
seeing anything. Rough sketches and throwaway experiments can start during research to settle
an uncertainty. Production screens and code follow a clear agreed contract. Branding has its
own stage; it need not hold up disposable prototypes or internal engineering experiments.

Research is broad, then selective in depth. Keep new products and dissenting feedback in the map.
Deepen a comparison when it can change a named decision; stop repeating research that yields no
new decision-relevant evidence. Never claim exhaustive coverage of all products or all opinions.
A polished image, a long document and an AI-generated persona are not proof of successful play.
