# Research 01 — market coverage and table needs

## Objective

Establish what is worth designing before building the new app. Reuse the earlier research as a
lead list, verify consequential claims, and broaden coverage of actual DM/player experiences.
Produce an evidence-backed direction, not a vote count or a promise to reproduce every product.

## Coverage

Inventory direct table/character tools, physical-table/offline companions, campaign and world
organization, rules/homebrew editors and tactical videogame interfaces. BG3 and D&D Beyond are
primary references; the other candidates are selected for relevant tasks, not popularity alone.

Starting candidates, not verified claims of current functionality: Foundry, Roll20, Fantasy
Grounds, Owlbear Rodeo, Encounter+, Shard, Alchemy, Kanka, LegendKeeper, World Anvil, Obsidian,
Notion, Homebrewery, GM Binder and Dungeon Master's Vault; BG3, Solasta, Divinity: Original Sin 2,
Pathfinder videogames, Pillars of Eternity and focused tactical/card interfaces where relevant.
Expand the map when a new category, useful counterexample or missing user need emerges. Record
bundled products separately. Do not buy access or claim firsthand use without it.

Cover DM preparation and live play; novice and experienced players; physical and digital dice;
phone, tablet, desktop and paper; offline and online sessions; creation, reading a sheet,
combat/interruptions, corrections, loot, scheduling, custom rules and accessibility. Include
Italian perspectives and compare them with English-language communities.

## Evidence collection

- Use official documentation and direct product observation for capabilities, version and behavior.
- Use public forum discussions, issue trackers, product reviews, guides and session accounts for
  experiences and preferences. Search praise, abandonment, workarounds and opposing opinions.
- For each material claim record source URL, access date, product/version, author role only when
  self-reported, context, observation/opinion/inference, corroboration, contradiction and the
  decision it could affect. Keep quotations short and source-linked; do not copy whole threads.
- Separate installations, active use, preference and observed task completion. Do not subtract
  overlapping module populations to invent an effects-only cohort. Counts from self-selected
  communities are not population shares or evidence about this specific group.
- Mark paywalls, unavailable versions and missing device/account access. Deduplicate syndicated
  articles and repeated accounts of the same incident. An unavailable source is a gap, not a fact.
- Preserve the existing nineteen product jobs as a starting inventory, including scheduling.
  Map each to observed problems and potential scenarios before recommending an order.

## Deliverables and exit

Create `evidence/research-01/coverage.md` (categories, candidates, coverage and gaps), `claims.md`
(claims with sources and counter-evidence), and `recommendation.md` (strongest needs, unresolved
assumptions, the proposed first scenario and design opportunities). Keep the recommendation short
and link its evidence; no new questionnaire or mandatory interview series.

The exit is adequate coverage of the named contexts, substantiation of every consequential
recommendation, explicit gaps and a separately reviewed proposal for the next scenario. Broaden
research when it can change a decision; otherwise deepen the chosen scenario. There is no fixed
number of sources that proves sufficiency and no claim to have read every opinion online.

Public opinion and automated scenario tests support autonomous progress. They do not establish
that real people successfully used our prototype. Human feedback can be incorporated opportunistically;
it is not a recurring prerequisite imposed on the owner's DM. Before a public release, disclose
which usability assumptions remain unobserved and propose a proportionate way to reduce them.
